# Dark Within Me: La Sua Origine

Written by David Gomez Valerio. English edition.

## Prologue: The Prism

Since the beginning of history there has been divinity, and there has been darkness. They have fought each other for ages, and the echo of their battles has shaken the whole universe more than once. Creatures of good and of evil, all of them born of the one supreme lord, the one who came to be called God the Father. First in the heavens, then on the earth, and at last in the shadows below, divinity and darkness raised their legions in battles without end. These beasts were given the name of Ikon: like the creatures of the earth, like gods, perfect, yet without reason.

At the hand of God, blessed with His divine power, two servants stood. At His left was Gyvenimas, ambassador of life and resurrection. At His right was Mirtis, guardian of God and funeral king of sin, darkness and death. Together they drew up a divine plan to end the endless wars between the great beasts. God the Father gave His will to the word of Gyvenimas, and with her power of life she gave God two children, heirs to the three realms and voices of peace among their legions. Both were made in His likeness, and yet they could not have been more different from each other. Their names were Vita and Éres.

Vita would reign over divinity. She would protect the balance and defend the creatures of light from extinction, and she would learn the wondrous art of giving life, to become queen of the heavens and ambassador to the earth. Éres would reign over darkness. He would bring justice, gather the souls of the beasts across the universe, and lock away in eternal penance any who defied his command. The abyss of other people's sins was his home, and the penance of souls darkened by evil became his own burden. He condemned every beast that would not obey the laws of God.

Gyvenimas grieved for Éres, who lived alone in the endless dark trying to master the abominable beasts of dread and malice. But Mirtis, born of the eternal darkness himself, taught Éres how to live among the shadows and how to tame the fiercest beasts. In time both Mirtis and Éres came to loathe the presence of Gyvenimas, and she returned to Vita's side.

For centuries divinity and darkness stayed at odds, though they came from the same origin. As the ages passed, both rulers began to grow old, for their bodies were imperfect, and each of them would need an heir to carry on the charge God the Father had given them.

Vita's body was like a woman's. She conceived from a sad sunflower seed and bore a son she named Herggott, child of Vita and heir of the heavens. That knowledge had been given to her by the great Gyvenimas, giver of life both brief and eternal. Éres's body was like a man's, and for all his reason he knew no way to bring forth an heir. Unable to please his Father, he felt only betrayal. Eaten alive by jealousy of his own sister, blind with the fear of failing his Father and convinced that He favored Vita, Éres challenged her to a fight to the death, so that she could never give their Father an heir either. He forgot the true charge God had given them, and the reason they had been made at all.

A great and terrible battle was fought across the cosmos, where both kinds fought to survive and for the rule of divinity and darkness. Vita accepted her brother's challenge, but in her heart she was only defending herself from his fury. She was never willing to truly face him. Even so, Éres, blinded by what he believed was treason, drove his black sword, dark as a river of shadow, into Vita's chest. He tore her Prism away from her, and with it, her life. Then he placed a seed of his own being inside her womb, and it bore fruit: a small boy, heir of the shadows, whom he named Reficul.

Éres went before the great walls of his Father's palace. His Father sees all things, and He already knew what had happened between His children. Only then did He understand that His son had been consumed by the darkness he had lived in for so long. Broken by the crime and by the tragedy between His children, God locked Éres in the deepest dungeons of hell, where he was never seen again.

Centuries later God created the heavens and the earth, and gave life to the plants and the animals as we know them. Meanwhile, in His small constellation, the wars began again. This time God believed the best way to stop the wars was to give the Ikon reason, and to treat them as equals. He took the counsel of Mirtis. He gave every Ikon the understanding of good and evil, and allowed them a body like that of God and of men, through an essence much like a soul, which He called the Prism.

Inside the Prism lived the essence that let an Ikon take a human body. It also held the true essence of its animal form: its old shape as a beast, which was given a life of its own and became its protector and companion. These wondrous creations were called gods, and that is when the God we know took the name of God the Father.

Gyvenimas was jealous of Mirtis, for she knew he had worked on the mind of Éres until he committed his unforgivable crime. She did not share God the Father's decision, and she chose to leave His side. Mirtis, instead, drew closer to Him, and demanded that Gyvenimas hand over the power of life and resurrection, and her place in the kingdom of heaven. Only then would he let her leave.

Gyvenimas looked God the Father in the eyes. Seeing no objection to Mirtis's demand, she drew a sealed scroll out of the air with her magic, opened it, and wrote on it the prayer that would grant the power of life and resurrection to anyone able to wield so much power. At the very moment Mirtis thought the scroll was his, Gyvenimas hurled it down toward the earth, where God had given life to humankind.

Mirtis seemed to lose his mind at her defiance, and he threw himself after the scroll without a second thought, forgetting the most important thing of all. The only gate between heaven and earth is a magical illusion. It opens only with a key, and it turns invisible for as long as it stays open, until it begins to close. By the time Mirtis came back to his senses, he had lost the trail of the scroll and forgotten where the gate was. He was forced to live in the world of men for long centuries...

## Chapter 1: Triangle of Misfortune

Tuscany, Italy. April 20, 1492. Many families of central Italy have gathered for a grand celebration: the birth of the firstborn of the D' San Fernald family.

Sir Cristian D' San Fernald, known everywhere as the remarkable Christian writer, was born in Rome in 1460. He began writing at fourteen, and his books spread across the whole of Europe. On one of his journeys to France, while presenting a book around Paris, he met the woman who would become his wife: Esther Giovenili.

Esther Giovenili was a sensual French singer and actress, known to her admirers as the Queen of Soprano. She was born in Lorraine in 1467 and sang as a girl in the Seraphim Choir of Paris. At nineteen she met Cristian D' San Fernald I. She fell in love with him, and they were married the following year.

Everyone waits impatiently for the parents to arrive and announce whether the child is a boy or a beautiful girl.

"There's the D' San Fernald carriage," said one of the guests.

"I'm dying to meet my nephew," said the lady's brother, Rosellio Giovenili.

The couple came before everyone, and one of the guests cried out with joy:

"It's a boy!"

"Congratulations! May God the Father bless the D' San Fernald family and their first son. Amen!" several guests chanted together.

"And what is his name, child?" asked the eldest of the guests.

"He will carry his father's name. He will be called Cristian D' San Fernald II," answered the mother.

And so the long-awaited celebration began: music, banquets, dancing and singing, a feast in every sense. Everyone admired the little Cristian and showered him with blessings.

"You've had a beautiful son. What a shame you'll have to give up singing to raise him," said Rosellio.

"Only for a few years, brother," Esther answered. "When Cristian is old enough to look after himself, I'll go back to the stage."

"I hope that beautiful voice of yours lasts forever, sister. Your songs will put your little prince to sleep in seconds," said Rosellio.

"Thank you, brother. I love you too," said Esther.

When the celebration ended, the guests began to leave the hall. Only the servants of the D' San Fernald residence remained, along with the stars of the evening.

"Can we go now?" said Cristian's father, in an arrogant tone.

"Is he always this stubborn?" asked Rosellio. "He's jealous even of your own brother."

"Please, Rosellio, you know how he is. Don't worry. He's a good husband, and I'm sure he'll be a great father," Esther answered.

"What you ought to do is get married and have a family and children of your own to look after, so you'd stop hovering over everyone else's," said Cristian's father.

"Someday I will. But I don't see what's wrong with being close to my sister," Rosellio replied.

The baby began to cry.

"Now look what you've done," Esther scolded them. "That's enough arguing. Cristian, we're leaving. Take care, Rosellio. I hope you have a safe trip back to Florence."

"Yes, do take care, brother-in-law. On such a lovely night, we wouldn't want anything to happen to you," said Cristian's father, mocking him.

"See you soon, Esther. Look after my nephew Cristian. I hope he takes nothing after his father," Rosellio answered.

"You wretch! How dare you say such a thing!" Cristian's father lunged at him, and his coachman had to hold him back.

"Stop it, please!" said Esther. "Cristian, let's go home. I'm not feeling well."

"All right, my love. We're going," he said.

It was easy to see that Cristian and Rosellio did not get along.

On the way back to the D' San Fernald residence, Esther sang beautiful love songs to little Cristian, who never stopped smiling at the glorious voice of his mother. His father smiled to see his firstborn laugh with joy. The D' San Fernald family felt blessed by the arrival of their first son.

***

London, England. May 11, 1495. The royal family of Siena, one of the most important cities of fifteenth-century Italy, was preparing to welcome the heir of the third generation of the crown of Siena. Its queen, Lady Carmen Peterwood, was at the side of her eldest daughter, Margaret Dwarson, who was only minutes away from giving birth to a baby girl. With them were Margaret's husband, Sir Giovanni Gloucester, patriarch of the Gloucester family of Durham, England, and her younger sister, Leonore Dwarson.

Everything seemed ready to welcome the first heir of the third generation to the throne of Siena. Lady Carmen could hardly wait to meet her first granddaughter, after so many years of waiting for one. For Giovanni, the birth of his daughter was more than a joy. It was his ticket to becoming king of Siena, and counsel to its kings, for many years to come.

Then the magic began. Every guest of honor had to leave the room where the child would be born. Only Queen Carmen had the right to stay at her daughter's side.

"Mother, I'm so frightened. Don't let go of my hand," said Margaret, gripping her mother's hand.

"Don't be afraid, child. I know. It's normal to be afraid the first time," said Lady Carmen calmly. "Everything will be fine, my dear. Very soon your daughter will be a beautiful princess."

"Thank you, Mother. You're so kind. May God the Father hear your words," said Margaret, a little calmer.

After hours of uncertainty, Lady Margaret gave birth to a beautiful girl, with golden hair and enormous sky-blue eyes, white as the snow that falls in winter on the fields of the lost city.

"Congratulations, my love, you did it. It's a beautiful girl you've brought into the world," said the queen.

"Thank you, Mother. She's beautiful. She reminds me so much of Grandmother Elizabeth," said Margaret.

"Forgive me, Your Highness. What name will this lovely girl carry?" asked the deacon of ceremonies, who would present her before God the Father.

"She will carry my grandmother's name. She will be called Elizabeth Gloucester Dwarson," Margaret answered.

Lady Carmen could not stop smiling, and neither could little Elizabeth, who held on tightly to her mother's fingers.

Once the room had been cleaned, the rest of the guests were allowed in to witness the happy event of the royal family of Siena.

"I can't believe it, sister. She looks exactly like Grandmother Elizabeth. It's incredible," said Leonore in amazement.

"That is exactly her name, my dear. Elizabeth, like my mother," said the queen.

"My love, won't you say a few words to me?" said Margaret.

"Of course, my love. Thank you for giving me the most beautiful daughter in all of Europe. I love you," said Duke Giovanni. "When you're out of here, I'll take you and our daughter to the most beautiful halls in Durham, so we can enjoy our little girl together."

"That's lovely. Well, I think we should leave you for a moment. There are things husband and wife will want to say in private," said the queen.

"Thank you, Mother. Truly, thank you for everything you've done for me," said Margaret.

"Just rest, my dear. There will be time for thanks," Lady Carmen answered.

When the guests had gone, only Giovanni and Margaret remained, with little Elizabeth, waiting for the new mother to recover.

"Lord Gloucester?" said the midwife. "Lord Gloucester!"

"Yes, yes. Tell me, what is it?" said Giovanni.

"You'll have to wait in the antechamber. Lady Gloucester needs to rest properly," said the midwife. "Your daughter may go with you, if you wish."

"Very well. That's no trouble at all," said Giovanni.

Duke Giovanni and his little Elizabeth waited in the antechamber all night, until the next morning. Then, all at once, loud screams of pain calling out her husband's name rang down the hall. Giovanni rushed to the room where his wife lay. The physician would not let the frantic husband in.

"What is happening? Let me in, damn you!" Giovanni shouted, fury in his eyes.

"Please understand, Lord Gloucester. We are dealing with the situation, and I beg you to let us continue," said the physician guarding the door.

Another scream of real agony came from Margaret. Desperate at his wife's cries, Giovanni knocked the useless physician down with a blow to the face and burst into the room.

"My love, what's happened? What is all this?" Giovanni cried.

"Please, my lord, we're doing everything we can to save her," said one of the nurses.

"Your wife is bleeding badly inside. We're trying to stop a hemorrhage caused by the birth," said another physician in the room.

The bed, the sheets and Margaret's body were soaked in blood. One look at the physicians' faces said her chances were very small. Giovanni ran to his wife's side, held her hand tightly and said: "Please, my love, hold on. Don't leave me, I beg you. Be strong."

"My love, don't let go of me, please," said Margaret, as her strength left her. "Take good care of our daughter. Make sure she's the happiest girl in the world when I'm gone."

"Why are you saying that, my love?" Giovanni cried. "My love? My love, my love, please, no!"

An unexpected turn struck the Gloucester family that morning. What had seemed a birth full of life and joy ended with the death of the princess, first in line to the royal crown of Siena. Duke Giovanni was utterly destroyed. He had lost the woman he loved and the mother of his daughter, and with her he had lost his chance to be declared king of Siena. With Margaret dead, the succession to the crown would pass to the next in line: Lady Leonore Dwarson.

After days of pain and sorrow, and after the body of the departed heir had lain in state before her most faithful subjects, she was buried in the front garden of the royal castle of Siena, where her nobility and beauty would live on forever in the hearts of those who knew her. There, friends and townsfolk gathered to say their last goodbye.

"I say goodbye to you, and with this farewell perhaps my most beautiful dream dies inside me. But I say goodbye to you for the rest of my life, though all my life I will keep thinking of you," said Lady Leonore Dwarson, sister of the departed.

"I do not fear your farewell. I fear the desolation that will flood my soul, knowing I will never again hold your hand or correct your mistakes. Never again," said Queen Carmen.

Weeping, pain, suffering and guilt flooded the garden with tears. Then a heavy rain began to fall from the grey skies around the castle. One by one the guests left, all except Giovanni, who stayed in the garden under the rain, in agony, in anguish and in rage at the cruel move fate had played on him.

"You took everything I had from my side. You tore away my chance to become king of Siena, to own endless riches," said Giovanni. "You robbed me of the only woman I ever loved, the woman for whom I gave up my parents' inheritance and the chance to be someone in this life, just to love her and be the king she needed for Siena."

"I curse you! I curse you, God the Father, as your own son once cursed you for betrayal!" Giovanni shouted, breaking into tears.

He fell to his knees on the wet ground, and Duke Giovanni began to lose his faith in God the Father. He never noticed that in that very moment his soul was slowly being corrupted by evil and darkness. A black cloud of horrible thoughts filled the mind of the Duke of Gloucester, patriarch of Durham, and he began to see life in a different light. He had only one purpose left: to take back what he swore was his by right. The royal crown of Siena.

## Chapter 2: The Last Song of Esther Giovenili

The loss of Siena's heir shook many regions of Europe and the wider world. Even in Tuscany, cradle of the D' San Fernald family, the news brought sadness to the hearts of those who knew her.

As the years went by, Cristian D' San Fernald II showed a gift for engineering and great skill with tools, just like his uncle Rosellio. But his father dreamed of seeing him become a great squire, so he took him to his close friend Henry Brasco, one of the best warriors, if not the very best, ever to serve in the armies of Italy.

The Brasco and D' San Fernald families were deeply loyal to each other. The two patriarchs shared a bond of friendship that had stayed unbroken through the years. Sir Henry Brasco was also known as the Hunter of the Black Gods. According to the historians, Sir Henry fought against the creatures of the shadows in the famous War between Light and Darkness, which seems impossible given how long ago that war took place. And yet, among the remains found on that battlefield, there was a sword bearing the crest of the Brasco family, with the name of Henry Brasco engraved on its golden blade. Whatever the truth, no opponent had ever defeated Sir Henry Brasco in battle, and he was thirty-five years old.

Sir Henry had a son of only two, named Marcus Brasco. With Cristian at his side, Henry loved teaching everything he knew about combat. Henry, his wife Helen, and even little Marcus thought of the D' San Fernald family as their own, and the friendship kept growing with every passing year.

One morning the D' San Fernald family received a letter. Count Sherman of Scotland, in Great Britain, cordially invited the wonderful singer to a celebration in honor of the engagement of his son, Anthony Sherman, to the young Greek countess Erika Rose. The former singer was moved and delighted that her art was still requested by great figures of royalty around the world. For her, and for Cristian's father, it was a wonderful chance to see new horizons, and to bring his books and her gift to all of Scotland. Without a moment's doubt, they began preparing for the unexpected journey. Unfortunately, Count Sherman had been clear: only the singer and her husband were invited to such a private ceremony. Young Cristian could not go.

"Can you believe it, darling? After almost fifteen years, your mother, the sensual Esther Giovenili, Queen of Soprano, returns to the stage. And what better place than the reception of one of the most powerful counts in Europe," Esther told her son.

"It's going to be a real spectacle, Mother. I hope this is the beginning of a new career," said young Cristian.

"I'm very sure of it, my love," said his father. "But hurry, we need everything ready for tomorrow morning."

"What? So soon? But I have important exams I can't miss," said the boy.

"I'm sorry, my love, but we can't take you with us to Scotland. It is the count's wish," said Esther.

"Uncle Henry will be in charge of you while we're away," said his father.

"Why can't Uncle Rosellio look after me?" said young Cristian.

"I'm sorry, love. The trip to Florence is too long. We wouldn't have time to make both journeys," Esther answered.

Young Cristian felt a little sad that he could not go with his parents on such a wonderful trip. However well the Brasco family treated him, he would rather have stayed with his uncle Rosellio.

A few days later, the moment of departure came. Cristian's parents said goodbye to their son and to the Brasco family as they boarded the train that would carry them north. The train to Scotland was about to leave.

"Take good care of yourself, my love. I'm already counting the hours until I'm back with you, my life," said Esther.

"Be respectful and brave, boy. Don't make me look bad in front of the Brasco family. Do you understand?" said Cristian I.

"Yes, Father," said young Cristian, lowering his head out of respect and fear.

"Come now, Cristian, your son is an excellent young man. I'm sure he won't give us any trouble. Will you, boy?" said Henry.

"No, sir!" answered young Cristian.

After the farewell, the Brasco family and young Cristian went back to the Brasco residence, where Henry prepared a room for his guest. Of all the visits young Cristian had made to the Brasco home, this would be the first time he spent the night.

During the days Cristian stayed with them, Sir Henry took the chance to push the boy's training harder. He already showed real maturity in the art of the sword and of defense. Strike after strike after strike, young Cristian handled the blade like a true warrior. Sir Henry was truly pleased, and surprised, by how well the boy mastered the sword and how fast he learned.

***

Meanwhile in Scotland, the artist couple made their entrance at Count Sherman's wedding celebration. Music, wine and every kind of delicacy filled every corner of the hall. Everywhere hung a beautiful poster showing the famous Queen of Soprano, Esther Giovenili. After several acts of dancing and juggling, and a showing of the magnificent books of Cristian D' San Fernald I, the long-awaited moment had finally come.

"Ladies and gentlemen, your attention, please. After fifteen years away from the stage, I present to you the incomparable, the one and only, the sensual Queen of Soprano, celebrated in Italy, in Paris, in Tuscany and in Egypt, and making her debut tonight in Scotland for the very first time. Ladies and gentlemen: Esther Giovenili, the Queen of Soprano!" announced the master of ceremonies over a drumroll.

The whole hall rose to its feet to greet the singer as she took the stage. Her magnificent voice captured the faces and the hearts of every guest. People who had known her voice long ago began to cry with joy at hearing their idol sing again. It was a beautiful, special night for everyone there.

When it was over, the triumphant couple walked hand in hand, celebrating their great success through the streets.

"Truly, my love, I had forgotten the magic of your songs. I love you," said Cristian I, as he danced with his beloved and kissed her.

"I'm so happy, my love. This is only the beginning of a new life. I can feel it," said Esther.

"You're the best singer in the world, Esther. You haven't lost your touch, darling," said Cristian I.

"And you still have the mind I fell in love with, Señor D' San Fernald," Esther answered.

The lovers walked all the way to the railway station, to take the train to the hotel where they would spend the night. On board, bound for Edinburgh, the capital of Scotland, Esther lifted the spirits of the other passengers with lively songs, while her husband clapped along and many of the passengers joined in the chorus. Even the driver sounded the great horn in rhythm to show how much he enjoyed it. Without anyone noticing, the train had picked up so much speed that it jumped its own rails and crossed onto the next track, the one carrying trains in the opposite direction.

After several minutes of music and dancing, another train coming down that track lit its lamps to full strength and sounded its emergency horn. By the time the driver of the D' San Fernalds' train saw it, there was almost no way to avoid the crash. With no other choice, the terrified driver pulled the brake, trying to stop. The track turned into a violent curve, and the train slid across the line sideways. Without slowing down, the oncoming train struck it right in the middle, destroying the central carriages and leaving both ends rolling across the ground. Dozens of lives were lost that night. The few who survived the collapse tried desperately to rescue their loved ones and their companions.

From one end of the wreck, Cristian I managed to crawl out of his carriage alive. Frantic and inconsolable, he searched the destruction for his beloved wife.

"Have you seen my wife? The singer, do you remember her? Have you seen her?" Cristian I asked the survivors who had escaped.

"You, sir, yes, you in the leather coat, have you seen my wife? The young lady in the blue dress, the one who was singing in the upper carriage. You've seen her, haven't you?" Cristian I demanded.

It got worse. The carriages began to catch fire, burning alive the survivors still trapped inside. Screams of pain filled the sky along with the thick clouds of smoke from the flames. Cristian did not lose hope. Grabbing a huge piece of metal, he began smashing the windows of the carriages, trying to get inside and reach his beloved. A burst of flame shot out of one of the broken windows and burned his left arm and part of his face. The young writer screamed in pain. Then a voice came from inside the train.

"My love, Cristian, I'm here! Please, help me!" Esther cried in desperation.

"My life, please hold on, I'll get you out of there!" Cristian I called back, just as desperate.

Cristian climbed into the train, terribly burned all over his body. Esther was pinned by one of the seats, her legs trapped beneath it. Cristian lifted his wife into his arms and tried to find a way out. Every door and every window was covered in fierce flames.

Then, as if by magic, a dimensional rift began to open inside the carriage. A faceless figure, like a specter, glowing with a violet light, began to step out of it. It was one of the creatures of darkness, making its unexpected appearance to offer them an evil bargain that would decide the fate of their lives forever.

"I've seen your face! You're one of the loyal servants of the dreaded Reficul. I've written about you, and I've read your name in a thousand texts, demon!" said Cristian's father, shielding his wife.

"I see you're quite the reader, writer. Then I suppose you know why I've come," the demon answered.

"That I don't know, demon. What could you want with a simple writer and his wounded wife?" said Cristian, fear in his eyes.

"Ahh, there you're wrong, writer. I do want something, and very much," said the demon, with hunger and thirst in its eyes.

"What do you want, creature of evil?" said Esther.

"Only to save your miserable lives. In exchange for the life of your son. Two lives for one. What do you say?" said the demon.

"You turned the tracks? You took the lives of hundreds of people for this?" Esther screamed.

"Very observant, my dear singer. But enough questions. Make your choice," the demon answered.

"I will never surrender my son's soul to your rule. Never!" cried Cristian I.

"Then you will die. And when you do, I will take your souls, along with the souls of the rest of the survivors," the demon answered in rage.

"There's no way out, my love. Please forgive me for saving your soul," said Cristian, his heart shattered.

In that moment Cristian took the pocket dagger he used to open his letters and drove it into Esther's chest, praying aloud:

*"I free you from the misfortune of being visited by a creature of darkness. I free your soul from the sins of others and your own, so that it may be received holy in the kingdom of God the Father. I cast out all the evil in you, and I guard your soul from being stolen. Amen."*

After these words, Cristian drew the dagger from his wife's chest and laid her empty body on the floor of the train. He knew mythology well, and the human sciences devoted to the study of the wondrous creatures of light and darkness. What Cristian knew was this: if he or his wife, after being visited by a demon, died at that demon's hands, it would be the same as pledging their souls to darkness. Their souls would never reach heaven, and they would be slaves of darkness in the next Arcana, if another one ever came.

"Very touching, writer. Taking the life of the woman you love to protect your son's soul," said the demon.

"I will never let you take my soul, or my family's, for your sacristy, demon," said Cristian, sure of himself.

He gripped the dagger again and swung it toward his own chest, but this time the demon was quicker. It hurled a gigantic ball of fire before the patriarch of the D' San Fernald family could take his own life. The young writer screamed in anguish and pain as his body was consumed by the fire and his soul burned from within. The demon would not stop laughing in triumph. Once the bodies of the D' San Fernalds had turned to ash, it stepped back into the dimensional rift, and it was never seen again.

The most remarkable thing about dimensional rifts is their hold on time. Every second in the real world lasts a full minute inside the space where one of these rifts stays open. That is why it is believed the D' San Fernalds died so quickly, and out of everyone's sight.
